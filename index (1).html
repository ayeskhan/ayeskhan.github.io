HackerFab design teamJun 2026 – presentFour-point probe lead

A four-point probe for a student-run fab

Problem

HackerFab is a student team at Waterloo building its own small semiconductor fab, with tools the team designs itself. The team could sputter aluminum films but had no way to check them. I took on the fab's first thin-film metrology tool and own it end to end.

Approach
  • Wrote the design proposal and validation plan before building: calibration against reference standards, repeatability, and uniformity mapping.
  • Designed the probe head in SolidWorks and 3D printed it for repeatable contact with the film.
  • Built the measurement chain: constant current through the outer probes, differential voltage across the inner pair, and current reversal to cancel thermal EMF.
Result

Validated the tool against reference resistors before trusting a single film reading.

Open problem: the sputtered films read anomalously high resistance. I'm using the probe to root-cause it and feeding results back to the deposition team.

Tools
  • SolidWorks
  • 3D printing
  • Measurement electronics
  • Sheet resistance
  • Validation
Microfabrication & Thin-Film TechnologySpring 2026UWaterloo cleanroom

MIS capacitors, bare wafer to electrical test

Problem

Build working Al/SiNₓ/p-Si capacitors on 3-inch silicon, then prove what every layer actually is.

Approach

Over three cleanroom sessions: RCA clean, PECVD silicon nitride at 350 °C, contact photolithography, CF₄/O₂ reactive ion etch, DC-sputtered aluminum, and PAN wet etch. Then characterized every layer and every device.

Result
  • Five identical capacitors within 2% of each other, leakage below 1 nA at +20 V.
  • Attributed the aluminum's higher resistivity and lower density to grain boundaries and voids in the as-deposited film.
  • Extended the C-V sweep to −40 V on the Boonton's internal bias to reach full accumulation.
Tools
  • PECVD
  • DC sputtering
  • Photolithography
  • RIE
  • Ellipsometry
  • C-V / I-V
Nanomaterials Synthesis LaboratorySpring 2026

Quantum-dot LEDs and nanoparticle synthesis

Problem

Control a quantum dot's colour through how it is grown, then turn the dots into a working device.

Approach

Synthesized CdSe quantum dots from precursors with a lab group, varying growth time, and built them into spin-cast quantum-dot LEDs.

Result
  • Tuned emission across the visible spectrum, relating particle size to colour with the Brus model using photoluminescence, UV-Vis, powder XRD, and SEM.
  • Also synthesized superparamagnetic iron-oxide nanoparticles by coprecipitation and Eu/Dy-doped phosphors, sizing crystallites by XRD line broadening.
