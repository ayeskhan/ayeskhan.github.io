HackerFab design teamJun 2026 – presentFour-point probe lead

A four-point probe for a student-run fab

HackerFab is a student team at Waterloo building its own small semiconductor fab: sputter deposition, maskless lithography, and device test, with tools the team designs itself. The team could deposit aluminum films but had no way to check them. I took on the fab's first thin-film metrology tool and own it end to end.

  • Designed a spring-contact probe head in SolidWorks and 3D printed it for repeatable contact with the film.
  • Built the measurement chain: constant-current sourcing through the outer probes, differential voltage sensing across the inner pair, and current reversal to cancel thermal EMF.
  • Wrote the design proposal and validation plan before building: calibration against reference standards, repeatability, and uniformity mapping. Validated against reference resistors before trusting a reading.
  • Now using it to diagnose anomalously high film resistance and feed results back to the deposition team.

  • 3D printing
  • Measurement electronics
  • Sheet resistance
  • Validation
Microfabrication & Thin-Film TechnologySpring 2026UWaterloo cleanroom

MIS capacitors, bare wafer to electrical test

Built Al/SiNₓ/p-Si capacitors on 3-inch silicon over three cleanroom sessions: RCA clean, PECVD silicon nitride at 350 °C, contact photolithography, CF₄/O₂ reactive ion etch, DC-sputtered aluminum, and PAN wet etch. Then characterized every layer and every device.

  • Attributed the aluminum's higher resistivity and lower density to grain boundaries and voids in the as-deposited film.
  • Extended the C-V sweep to −40 V on the Boonton's internal bias to reach full accumulation.
